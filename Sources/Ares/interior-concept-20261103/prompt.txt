Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: production environment concept / visual prototype for the existing Ares multiplayer FPS map.
Primary request: Redesign this very basic box-built interior into a distinctive, believable Martian atmosphere-processing facility. Produce ONE polished, wide 16:9 environment image that an environment artist can use as a practical visual target.

Input images: Image 1 is the existing main hall, a layout and identity reference only. Image 2 is a fresh screenshot of the current sparse hall floor, a reference for the boxy placeholder equipment that needs a stronger design language. Image 3 is the existing side wing, a reference for the repeated flat construction. These are context references, not pixels to preserve. Generate a coherent new interpretation, not a collage or before/after sheet.

Keep recognizable: the tall orange-lit cylindrical atmosphere processor at the centre; its elevated ring catwalk and bridges to perimeter galleries; ground-level circulation around it; side stairs and pressure-door connections; the tall sloping observation windows looking onto the red Martian landscape. Retain roughly the existing room size, two levels, route arrangement and human scale. Keep the walkable routes open and intelligible.

Design direction: grounded aerospace-industrial architecture with a strong relationship between structure and machinery. Replace flat rectangular wall stretches with deep structural bays, rounded pressure-wall haunches, coved transitions and recessed removable service panels. Give the high ceiling a clear rhythm of substantial curved portal ribs with purposeful longitudinal cable trays and ducts. Give gallery fascias depth through folded metal sections, recessed light channels, real brackets and visible load paths. Use curves and tapered/chamfered forms deliberately, balanced against clean planar surfaces.

The processor should read as engineered hardware: restrained orange glow behind segmented glass, layered concentric service collars, bolted inspection housings, insulated pipe connections and a grounded base manifold. Replace scattered generic boxes with a small number of designed waist-high process units: horizontal pressure vessels, compact pump skids with formed rounded cowls and angled control stations. Cover positions remain near the original locations; avoid introducing new walls or obstructing stairs and lanes. Avoid filling the floor with props.

Materials: warm off-white pressure panels, graphite structural steel, muted oxide-red accents, brushed stainless fittings, dark rubber seals, anti-slip floor plates and inset utility trenches. Use believable thicknesses, rounded manufactured edges and localised wear. Keep broad surfaces calm; details concentrate at joints, access points and service interfaces. Yellow/black warnings only at actual hazards and a few stair/threshold edges.

Composition: a single wide player-eye-height three-quarter view from one side of the hall, showing the processor, a broad readable foreground route, upper galleries, structural ceiling and Martian windows. Environment only, no people, weapons, HUD or overlays.
Lighting: legible neutral-white work lighting on walls and paths, warm amber spill from the processor, soft cooler ambient fill and a subdued rust-coloured exterior. Rich but controlled contrast, retain detail in shadow, no blown-out reactor, excessive bloom or thick fog.
Style: high-end realistic real-time game environment concept, tactile and buildable, coherent art direction rather than decorative sci-fi noise.
Text: only small naturally integrated existing labels where needed: "ARES FACILITY", "ATMOS PROCESSING", "SECTOR 03".
Avoid: plain cube props, endless identical square tiles, hazard stripes on every edge, black featureless boxes, generic space-station corridor kits, random greebles, floating or unsupported architecture, fantasy cathedral scale, holograms, neon strips everywhere, glossy wet floors, logos from other games, watermarks.
